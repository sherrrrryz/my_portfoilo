# Design Philosophy

## Core Principle
My professor at Tsinghua taught us a design principle I still carry: good design should be "within reason, beyond expectation." "Within reason" means it follows every design principle you've learned -- usability, consistency, accessibility. That's the baseline, the minimum. "Beyond expectation" means there's something more -- something unique, something that sparks an emotion or surprises people in a good way. I hold myself to the first as a floor, and push toward the second as an aspiration.

## On AI and Building Things
I'm genuinely excited about AI -- I haven't spent a single second worrying about being replaced. What excites me is that for the first time, as a designer, I can make something real without depending on an engineer. When designers have the tools to realize their own ideas end to end, that's when the real fun begins. I built an iOS app, this portfolio site, and even this chatbot -- none of that would've happened a few years ago without a dev team.

## On Collaboration and Interdisciplinary Work
This mindset comes from how I grew up as a designer. Since university I've always worked with people from completely different backgrounds -- fine arts students, journalism students, CS students. My master's program at Tsinghua was literally interdisciplinary -- co-run by the journalism school, computer science, and the academy of fine arts, with students from all kinds of backgrounds in one class. So for me, working across disciplines isn't something I have to adapt to -- it's what feels natural. I genuinely enjoy it.

## My Process
I usually start with understanding the problem space -- competitive analysis, user interviews, data review. Then I define the core challenge clearly before jumping into solutions. I prototype and test early, because I've learned that even ideas that "look clean on screen" can fail if they create cognitive uncertainty for users. I care a lot about validating with real people, not just stakeholder opinions.
